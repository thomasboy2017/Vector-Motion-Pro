Quick Guide

New here?

A compact vector and keyframe animation studio for learning the fundamentals. It is not a replacement for Adobe Animate or Illustrator.

60-second start

  1. Press R, drag on the canvas to draw a rectangle.
  2. Press V, click it, and change the fill or add a gradient in Design.
  3. Go to Motion and press ◆ Add keyframe at frame 0.
  4. Drag the playhead to 2s, then drag the object. A tween is created automatically.
  5. Press Play, then Export — or open Preview.

Pro touches

  • Shift-click to multi-select, then align/distribute.
  • Alt-drag duplicates; Shift constrains while moving or resizing.
  • Snap shows guides when edges or centres line up.
  • Onion skin ghosts the previous and next keyframes.
  • Ctrl+K opens the command palette.

Vecta Motion Pro — Documentation

Getting started

Vecta Motion Pro is a small vector drawing and keyframe-animation tool that runs entirely in your browser. It is intentionally simple and experimental — a place to learn the core ideas of vector design and animation.

Important: this is a hobbyist/experimental project. It is not meant to replace Adobe Animate or Adobe Illustrator. Expect rough edges, and save your work as a project JSON file regularly.

The interface

  • Left toolbar — selection and drawing tools.
  • Canvas — the artboard. Everything you draw lives here.
  • Right inspector — Design, Layers, Motion and Guide panels.
  • Bottom timeline — frames, keyframes and playback.

Your first drawing

  1. Press R and drag on the canvas to draw a rectangle.
  2. Press V, click the rectangle, and change its fill colour in the Design tab.
  3. Press E for an ellipse, S for a star, P for a polygon.
  4. Use the Layers tab to change stacking order, visibility and locking.

Your first animation

  1. Select an object.
  2. Open Motion and click ◆ Add keyframe (this captures the object at the current frame).
  3. Drag the playhead in the timeline to about 2 seconds.
  4. Move or resize the object on the canvas. A new keyframe is created automatically.
  5. Press Play. The object now tweens between the two keyframes.
Autosave: every change is mirrored to this browser's local storage. If you close the tab, your last session is restored automatically. Use Save to download a real project file.

What's new in Pro

  • Multi-select — Shift-click objects, then move, resize, duplicate, align or distribute them.
  • 8 resize handles + rotation handle — hold Shift to preserve aspect ratio or snap rotation to 15°.
  • Snapping guides — edges and centres align to the artboard and other objects. Toggle in the menu.
  • Gradients — linear two-colour fills with adjustable angle.
  • Effects — drop shadows, blur and CSS blend modes.
  • Star & polygon tools with points/sides and inner radius controls.
  • Brush & eraser — freehand polyline strokes plus one-click object removal.
  • PSD / AI / PDF import — best-effort raster layers or first-page render.
  • Tabbed inspector — Design, Layers, Motion and Guide panels.
  • Multi-line text with alignment, line height and letter spacing.
  • Onion skin — ghost the previous and next keyframes while animating.
  • Motion tools — copy, paste and reverse keyframes; auto-key can be switched off.
  • Timeline zoom and keyframe retiming — drag diamonds to change when they happen.
  • Playback modes — loop, once, or ping-pong.
  • Preview window — full-screen playback without the editor chrome.
  • Sprite-sheet export, JPEG/WebP stills, and improved video rendering.
  • Command palette (Ctrl+K) for fast access to every action.
  • Autosave to local storage plus sample scene.

Vector graphics basics

Vector graphics describe shapes with maths (positions, curves, fills) instead of pixels. That means they can be scaled without becoming blurry. This app stores every object as a vector shape.

Shapes

  • Rectangle — width, height and corner radius.
  • Ellipse — defined by its bounding box.
  • Line — a straight stroke.
  • Star — adjustable point count and inner radius.
  • Polygon — adjustable side count.
  • Brush — a freehand polyline stroke (editable like any object).
  • Text — live text, now multi-line.
  • Image — a raster image placed on the artboard (still scales, but is not vector).

Fill, stroke and opacity

Fill can be a solid colour, a linear gradient or none. Stroke is the outline colour; its width is in pixels, and it can be dashed. Opacity controls transparency.

Stacking order

Objects are drawn from back to front. The Layers tab lists the front-most object at the top. Use Bring forward / Send backward or drag rows to reorder.

Transforms

Every object has X, Y, width, height, rotation, scale X and scale Y. Rotation and scale happen around the object’s centre. Drag the corner or edge handles to resize, or the round handle above the box to rotate.

Animation basics

Animation is a sequence of still images shown quickly. In this app you don’t draw every frame; you set a few keyframes and the software fills in the frames between them.

Frames, FPS and duration

  • Frame — one moment in time.
  • FPS — frames per second. 24–30 FPS feels smooth; 12 FPS feels choppier and more stylised.
  • Duration — total length of the timeline in seconds.

Keyframes

A keyframe stores an object’s animated values at a specific frame: position, size, rotation, scale, opacity, fills and gradient colours. Diamonds in the timeline show where keyframes are. Drag a diamond sideways to retime it.

The auto-key rule

Once an object has at least one keyframe, editing that object creates or updates a keyframe at the current playhead position. This is what makes quick tweening possible. Objects with no keyframes stay static. You can switch auto-key off in the Motion panel.

Playback

Use the play button, the Space bar, or the timeline play button. Choose Loop, Once or Ping-pong in the timeline bar. Scrub the playhead by clicking or dragging the ruler.

Tweening in this app

Tweening means generating the in-between values automatically. Select an object with at least two keyframes, and the app interpolates position, size, rotation, scale, opacity and colour.

Make a bouncing ball

  1. Draw an ellipse.
  2. At frame 0, add a keyframe.
  3. Move the playhead to about 0.6s and move the ball down.
  4. Move the playhead to about 1.2s and move it back up.
  5. Set the first keyframe’s easing to Ease out and the middle one to Ease in.
  6. Press Play.

Easing

Each keyframe has an easing setting that controls the segment that starts at that keyframe:

  • Linear — constant speed.
  • Ease in / out — slow start or slow finish.
  • Ease in-out — slow at both ends (good for smooth movement).
  • Cubic variants — stronger acceleration.
  • Ease out back — overshoots slightly, useful for playful motion.

Change easing in the Motion tab’s keyframe list. The tween updates instantly. Use Copy motion and Paste motion to move a whole animation onto another object, or Reverse to flip it.

If you move the playhead and edit a tweened object, a new keyframe is inserted at that frame. This is normal and lets you refine the motion. Turn off Auto-key if you only want to adjust the object without recording a keyframe.

Gradients & effects

Linear gradients

Select an object and choose Linear gradient under Fill type. Pick the two colours and an angle. Gradients export cleanly to SVG and PNG, and animate with keyframes if you keyframe the colours.

Drop shadows and blur

Effects are rendered with SVG filters. Shadows use X/Y offset, blur radius, colour and opacity. A global blur softens the whole object. Both effects survive SVG export; very large blur values can be slow on very large artboards.

Blend modes

Blend modes map to CSS mix-blend-mode and work in the editor and browser-rendered exports. Some PDF or older SVG viewers may ignore them.

Effects stack with opacity. A screen-blended glow shape on top of a dark background is a quick way to fake lighting.

Import & export

Images

PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF and SVG files can be imported as image objects. Imported SVGs are treated as a single image, not as editable separate shapes. Images are embedded in the project JSON, so files can get large.

PSD (Photoshop)

PSD import uses the open-source ag-psd decoder loaded from a CDN. Simple documents with raster layers usually import as separate image layers. Layer masks, smart objects, adjustment layers, text layers and some blend modes may be flattened, missing or inaccurate.

PSD import is best-effort. For important work, export a PNG or SVG from Photoshop first, or rebuild the artwork with vector shapes here.

AI (Illustrator)

Most modern .ai files are PDF-compatible. This app tries to render the first page as an image using PDF.js. That means the artwork arrives as a raster image, not editable paths. Very old EPS-only .ai files are not supported.

Best workflow: in Illustrator choose File → Export → SVG, then import that SVG here (or use PNG). It keeps the best quality.

Project files

Save downloads a .json project containing objects, keyframes, canvas settings and timeline settings. Open restores it. The browser also keeps an autosave copy.

Exporting

  • SVG — true vector export of the current frame.
  • PNG / JPEG / WebP — raster export of the current frame, with scale and transparency options.
  • Sprite sheet — every timeline frame packed into a PNG grid. Useful for games.
  • MP4 H.264 / H.265 — available only if your browser’s MediaRecorder supports it. Chrome/Edge are the best bet. H.265 is rare.
  • WebM (VP9/VP8) — widely supported fallback.

Video export renders the timeline frame by frame. Audio is not supported. Very long animations or huge canvases may be slow. Video timing follows real time, so keep the tab visible while exporting.

Limitations & honest notes

This program is designed for experimental / hobbyist use. It is not meant to replace Adobe Animate, Adobe Illustrator, After Effects or other professional tools.
  • No native PSD/AI editing. PSD is decoded best-effort; AI is usually rasterised through PDF.
  • No masks, symbols, bones, audio, or boolean path operations.
  • No groups: multi-select, align and distribute are the grouping substitutes.
  • Text is single-style per object; no rich text runs.
  • No collaborative editing or cloud storage. Work lives in this browser's local storage and in files you save.
  • Video export uses the browser’s MediaRecorder. MP4/H.265 support varies by browser and platform. Timing may not be perfectly frame-accurate.
  • Large PSD files, huge images or very long timelines can be slow or run out of memory.
  • This is a learning tool. Save your project as JSON often.

Recommended browser

Chrome or Edge on desktop for the best video export support. Firefox and Safari can draw and animate, but video formats may be limited to WebM or unavailable.
